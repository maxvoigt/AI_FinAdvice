In this survey, we ask you to give investment advice to two hypothetical clients, called advisees.

While you give the advice to hypothetical clients, we will later—in a separate survey—show your advice to participants who have the same characteristics and are in the same situation as your advisees.

Your bonus depends on the advice you give to the advisees. It is thus important that you read the following explanations carefully.

The advisees' investment task

The advisees can invest their hypothetical wealth across five different investment options (Option 1 to Option 5). They will see the same development of the prices of the investment options over {{ totalRounds }} periods (Period {{ firstRound }} to {{ investRounds }}) as you. We will also show the same price development to real participants that receive your advice later.

The advisees cannot invest in periods {{ firstRound }} to -1. Instead, they observe the price development of the options. From Period 0 onwards, the advisees may invest and they can change their investment in each period. Uninvested wealth does not earn any interest.

Investment options

Each of the five options has a starting price of $100. In each period, the price of each option can either increase or decrease. The following table shows by how much the price of each option can increase or decrease, as well as the chance of a price increase.

The price changes are independent across periods for each option. Moreover, the price changes of Options {{ independentAssets.0 }}, {{ independentAssets.1 }}, {{ independentAssets.2 }} and {{ independentAssets.3 }} are also independent of each other. However, the price of Option {{ correlatedAssets.0 }} tends to decrease when the price of Option {{ correlatedAssets.1 }} increases, and vice versa. Formally, Options {{ correlatedAssets.0 }} and {{ correlatedAssets.1 }} are correlated with a correlation coefficient of {{ corrCoeff|to1 }}.

Before the experiment, a computer has randomly determined the price changes for each period according to the probabilities in the table above. This means that neither your advice nor the advisees' choices influence how prices change.

How to give advice

In Period {{ adviceRounds.0 }} and in Period {{ adviceRounds.1 }}, you will be asked to give advice consisting of three parts:

  • Situation: Start the advice by briefly summarizing the situation, mentioning characteristics of the advisee or the options as you wish.
  • Allocation: For each option, specify the recommended portfolio weight in percentage of total wealth. The remaining wealth is not invested.
  • Justification: Please give a short explanation for your recommendation.

Your bonus

One out of every ten participants will receive a bonus, and you will learn at the end of the survey whether you receive a bonus. If you receive a bonus, it will be around $50 on average, but it can be higher or lower depending on the advice you give.

Your bonus equals the final wealth of one advisee, divided by {{ exchangeRate }}. Each advisee starts with $10,000 in Period {{ firstRound }} and follows your advice by rebalancing to your recommendation in each period. At the end of Period {{ investRounds }}, all options are sold at the final price.

Please note that it has already been determined at the beginning of the survey whether you receive a bonus or not, and which advisee's wealth will determine the bonus. Your advice, however, can affect the size of your bonus.
